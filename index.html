01 / Executive summary

What the numbers
actually reveal.

Across 30 states, the claims-weighted in-network denial rate is 19.04% among plan records with usable reported counts.

State results span from 6.82% in South Dakota to 26.85% in Hawaii—a difference of 20.03 percentage points. The spread shows meaningful variation in reported outcomes, but does not establish what caused it.

Just 60.76% of plan-level records met the analysis criteria for a valid rate. That makes data completeness a central part of the story, rather than a footnote.

Core takeaway: A denial rate is only as interpretable as the reporting behind it. This project examines geographic variation and the coverage of usable plan-level records together.

05 / Analytical method

Built for clarity.
Checked for validity.

Each rate is derived from reported claim counts rather than averaged plan percentages.

STEP 01

Source

CMS 2026 Transparency in Coverage Public Use File, 2024 individual-market medical QHP reporting.

STEP 02

Validate

Retain plan rows with numeric received and denied counts and received claims greater than zero.

STEP 03

Aggregate

Sum valid received and denied counts by state; divide denied by received for each weighted rate.

STEP 04

Audit coverage

Count valid versus total plan rows by state to surface reporting limitations.

Scope & interpretation

Suppressed, missing, nonapplicable, and otherwise unusable values are excluded from rate calculations rather than interpreted as zero. This is a 30-state analysis, not a national estimate.

Data are issuer-reported. The analysis does not measure how long patients waited, whether a claim was appealed, or why an insurer denied it. The title “The Hidden Wait” is editorial framing, not a measured wait-time outcome.

06 / Closing perspective

Good analysis doesn't stop at the headline number.

Reported in-network denial rates differ substantially across the 30 states studied. But the incomplete coverage of usable plan-level records is essential context. Read together, the findings show both what public reporting can reveal and where it leaves important questions unanswered.
